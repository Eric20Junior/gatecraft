# Trademark policy

Gatecraft is licensed under [MIT](LICENSE), which grants you very broad rights over
the **code and documents** — use, modify, redistribute, sell, fork. Its only
condition is that you keep the copyright and permission notice.

MIT says nothing about names, and that is not an oversight in this policy: a
copyright licence and a trademark are different rights. Granting you the first
does not grant you the second, and trademark rights exist whether or not a
licence mentions them.

"Gatecraft", the Gatecraft logo, and confusingly similar variants are our
trademarks. This page explains what that means in practice, because a permissive
licence tends to leave people guessing about the name.

The rule behind everything below is one sentence: **it must be clear who made
what.** Nobody should install something believing it came from us when it did
not, and nobody should hesitate to talk about Gatecraft for fear of a legal letter.

## You do not need permission to

- **Use Gatecraft**, privately or commercially, and say that you do.
- **Say your project uses, supports, integrates with, or is built on Gatecraft.**
  Nominative use — referring to us by our actual name to describe a true
  relationship — needs no licence and we will not object to it.
- **Write about Gatecraft**: tutorials, reviews, comparisons, conference talks,
  criticism. Favourable or not.
- **Teach or train with it**, including paid courses and consulting where Gatecraft
  is part of what you deliver.
- **Fork the repository** and keep the name in unmodified files, history, and
  attribution notices. MIT requires you to preserve the copyright notice, and
  doing so is not a trademark violation.
- **Distribute Gatecraft unmodified** — mirror it, vendor it, bundle it into an
  internal platform — under its own name.
- **Use the word in ordinary prose**: "we standardised on Gatecraft", "ported our
  Gatecraft setup to the new repo".

## You need permission to

- **Name a product, service, or company** "Gatecraft", or anything close enough to be
  mistaken for it.
- **Publish a package** called `gatecraft`, or a name that reads as an official
  variant — `gatecraft-pro`, `gatecraft-cloud`, `@yourorg/gatecraft`.
- **Use the logo** as your product's mark, or as anything other than a reference
  to us.
- **Describe your offering as "official"**, "certified", "endorsed by", or
  "powered by Gatecraft" in a way that implies we vetted it.
- **Register** "Gatecraft" or a confusingly similar mark, in any jurisdiction.
- **Use it as a domain name** where the domain is plausibly ours —
  `gatecraft.io`, `get-gatecraft.com`, `gatecraft-docs.dev`.

Ask: **trademark@gatecraft.dev**. Reasonable requests are usually granted, in
writing, at no cost.

## Naming a fork

MIT gives you an unconditional right to fork. It does not give you our name for
the result.

**Renaming does not mean erasing where it came from.** The licence obliges you to
keep the copyright notice — "Copyright (c) 2026 Gatecraft" — in copies and
substantial portions, so the origin travels with the code even after a rename.
Stripping it is a licence violation, not a rebrand.

Beyond that minimum, MIT does not force you to credit us, and we are not going to
pretend it does. Saying where a fork came from is a courtesy rather than a
requirement — but it is the courtesy that lets your users find the upstream, and
lets ours find you. We ask for it and we do not enforce it.

**Works:**

- `wren` / `ledgerloop` / `northstar-eng` — your own name, no relationship implied
- "Wren, a fork of Gatecraft"
- "Wren — based on Gatecraft"
- "Wren is compatible with Gatecraft `.ai/` layouts"

**Does not work:**

- `Gatecraft Plus`, `Gatecraft Enterprise`, `Gatecraft Community Edition`
- `MyCorp Gatecraft`
- `gatecraft-extended` on npm
- Anything using our logo as its own
- A fork with the copyright notice deleted — that is a licence breach regardless
  of what you called it

The distinction is whether a reader would think we published it. "A fork of
Gatecraft" describes a relationship. "Gatecraft Plus" claims to *be* Gatecraft.

If your fork is substantial and you would rather contribute it back, we would
rather that too — see [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md).

## Extensions, plugins, and integrations

Naming an add-on after the thing it extends is normal and we support it, in the
`<your-name> for Gatecraft` form:

- ✅ `terraform-standards for Gatecraft`
- ✅ `gatecraft-django-standards` — descriptive prefix on a clearly third-party package
- ❌ `Gatecraft Terraform` — reads as an official first-party module

If your package is on a registry, say plainly in the README that it is
community-maintained and not an official Gatecraft package. One sentence is enough, and
it resolves nearly everything this page exists to prevent.

## Enforcement

We are not looking to police conversation. In practice we act only where real
confusion about origin exists — someone passing their work off as ours, or ours
as theirs.

If we think something crosses the line, the first contact will be an email asking
you to change it, with time to do so. Legal escalation is a last resort and has
never yet been necessary.

If you are unsure whether your use is fine, ask before you launch. It is a short
email and it is much cheaper than a rename.

**trademark@gatecraft.dev**
